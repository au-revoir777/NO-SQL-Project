## 1. Diagnosis

The tests that back the paper's positive claims survive the checks a reviewer would apply: stratified tests and whole-run bootstrap. Three things a reviewer would still flag:
1. **Pooled means hide scenario heterogeneity in every main comparison.** That includes the scenario C1 is about.
2. **Five "no difference" statements are absences of significance**, and with a declared margin only two of them would pass an equivalence test.
3. **The paper runs about 28 tests with Holm only inside three local tables.** Its test seeds were reused across about 20 experiments, some designed after earlier test results were seen.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Independence and heterogeneity (Table II pools 5 scenarios × 10 seeds)

Pooling 50 scenario-seed runs into one Wilcoxon test treats them as exchangeable. They are not: the paired difference depends on scenario (one-way ANOVA on paired differences, p ≤ 0.032 in every main comparison).

**This is the most consequential S1 finding.** On `hub_heavy`, the scenario built to test hub hiding, the full pipeline is not distinguishable from the scored cap-10 rules. Its advantage comes from the noisy, drifting and adversarial scenarios. Combined with N1 (rule recall does not depend on the cap), the evidence locates the gain in precision and recall under benign noise, not in recovering rings hidden behind hubs. Against cap 100 + scorer, hub_heavy is +0.12 [0.01, 0.23], so the low cap helps the rules there, or the high cap hurts them.

The ablation "w/o hub weighting" is not a null result: pooled +0.12 is the average of opposite-sign scenario effects (hub_heavy +0.39, adversarial +0.22, baseline 0.00, high_noise −0.13; heterogeneity p = 0.005). "Not significant" (Table V) and "no difference" are both the wrong summary. The right one is "scenario-dependent".

The stratified bootstrap with 5 seeds per stratum is anti-conservative. For that ablation it gives [0.04, 0.20], against [−0.01, 0.25] for the t-interval. I recommend t-intervals or a cluster bootstrap at n ≥ 10 per stratum, not 5-seed strata.

### 2.2 Absence of significance read as equivalence

The proposed margin is **δ = 0.05 F1**. The justification is data-independent: a run has 4.5 true rings on average (Table II streams), so one ring found or missed moves that run's F1 by about 0.1–0.2. δ = 0.05 is about one ring in every two to four runs, below what an analyst queue would notice.

### 2.3 Power

Paired runs. MDE is the smallest true difference detectable with 80% power at two-sided α 0.05 (t approximation). "n for TOST" is the runs needed to show equivalence at δ 0.05 with 80% power when the true difference is 0.

| Comparison | n now | SD of Δ | MDE now | n for TOST (δ 0.05) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proposed vs cap 10 + scorer | 50 | 0.181 | 0.073 | 112 |
| Tuned vs a-priori | 50 | 0.155 | 0.063 | 83 |
| Hub-weighting ablation | 20 | 0.281 | 0.186 | 272 |
| Time-decay ablation | 20 | 0.129 | 0.086 | 58 |
| Hysteresis ablation | 20 | 0.053 | 0.035 | 10 |
| Constant vs shipped (5×10) | 50 | 0.155 | 0.062 | 82 |
| Constant vs shipped (hub_heavy) | 30 | 0.165 | 0.087 | 94 |
| 1/√d vs shipped (5×10) | 50 | 0.072 | 0.029 | 18 |
| Incremental vs 1 s timer | 25 | 0.140 | 0.082 | 68 |
| Incremental vs full, 500 hub | 5 | 0.094 | 0.157 | 31 |
| Constant fan-out vs full, 500 hub | 5 | 0.097 | 0.162 | 33 |

Consequences:
- **Exact paired tests cannot reach p < 0.05 at 5 seeds.** The smallest two-sided Wilcoxon p at n = 5 is 0.0625, and at n = 3 it is 0.25. So no 5-seed or 3-seed comparison in the paper (hub sizes, large hubs, sweeps, scale) can be significant whatever the effect.
- **The hub-weighting ablation would need about 270 runs** for equivalence, and its effect is heterogeneous anyway. Report it per scenario instead.

### 2.5 Selection leakage

Checked against the seed sets in every `results/*/results.csv`:
- **Thresholds and weights are clean.** Every tuned value comes from files with seeds 301–303 only (`tuning`, `e1b_tuning`, `e2b_tuning`, `e2d_tuning`, `e13_tuning`). No test table or figure uses 301–303. The one exception is by design and disclosed: the RQ7 ring-level calibrator is fitted on 301–303 and evaluated on 1001–1010 (e10 protocol). The scorer uses 101–110 and is tested on 201–205.
- **Fig. 4's "best F1" is an oracle operating point.** For both arms it is the alert threshold with the highest F1 *on the test runs* (1001–1005). The bootstrap re-selects it in every resample, so the interval accounts for the selection, but the point estimates are optimistic for both arms. The caption should say "best F1 over the threshold sweep on the test runs (oracle)". The bootstrap resamples whole runs (scenario × seed), 2,000 times, stratified by scenario (seed 0).
- **Test seeds were reused across experiments.** Seeds 1001–1010 (or 1001–1005) appear in about 20 experiments. Several were designed after earlier test results were seen: E2d (per-detector thresholds) from Table V; E2b/E2c (weight functions) from the ablation; E5b's bound rule. That is a garden of forking paths on one seed set. The confirmatory runs (§2.4) should use fresh seeds 2001+, declared before running, as rule 11 requires.

## 3. Analysis specs (no new runs)

1. **Table II:** add a per-scenario panel of paired Δ (95% t, W/T/L) against the strongest scored baseline. Report the pooled estimate as the scenario-weighted mean with a cluster bootstrap over seeds within scenario (10 seeds per stratum), and keep the Wilcoxon only as stratified (van Elteren or within-scenario sign-flip).
2. **Equivalence:** once δ is confirmed, TOST (paired t, α 0.05 each side) for the no-difference claims in §2.2, in a separate equivalence family (Holm if more than one is retained in the abstract or conclusion).
3. **n ≤ 5:** report min–max over seeds and exact counts; no p-values.
4. **Fresh-seed confirmatory runs (later items):** size them from §2.3. For R1 (the pairwise detector ± cap, paired), the SD of comparable contrasts is 0.15–0.28. Detecting 0.10 F1 with 80% power needs 18–62 runs per contrast, so plan 50 (5 scenarios × seeds 2001–2010). TOST at δ 0.05 needs 77–270 runs, too many to plan; R1 should therefore be a superiority test, with an equivalence claim only if the CI happens to fall inside the margin.

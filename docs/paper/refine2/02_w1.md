### 0c. Why

At the first batch where full finds each missed ring and incremental does not, I compared the two views (`scripts/diagnostics/peel_compare.py`):

| Case | Ring members in incremental view | Thresholded component holding the ring (inc / full) | Incremental candidate |
|---|---|---|---|
| log 1002, batch 70 | 1 of 4 | 389 / 447 users | none |
| log 1002, batch 72 | 3 of 4 | 421 / 441 | none |
| const 1001, batch 91 | 3 of 5 | 442 / 525 | none |
| const 1004, batch 74 | **4 of 4** | 462 / 514 | none |
| const 1005, batch 87 | 4 of 5 | 517 / 539 | none |

Two mechanisms, neither the one §III-C states:

1. **Partial region.** In 4 of 5 cases no ring member was touched in that batch (`seeds_in_cand = 0`), so the ring entered the region only partly through other users' expansion. The missing members are linked to the ring by small attributes (degree 2–4) and transfers, not only through the hub.
2. **Peeling is not local.** At θ_e the pair graph of these streams has one giant component (389–539 users), and the ring sits inside it. Greedy peeling to density 0.75 and size 3–25 depends on the whole component, so it can return the ring on the full graph and not on the region's sub-component, **even with every ring member present** (const 1004).

What this means:
- **§III-C's explanation is refuted for these runs.** It says a constant "lets one busy attribute reach the threshold". In every listed pair the 500-user hub alone gives 0.94–0.96 (constant) or 0.16 (shipped), below θ_e. Pairs pass only with transfers or a second attribute.
- **The constant's extra misses are not shown to come from the 250 limit.** The bound rule changed nothing (15/15), and the misses come from the region/peel interaction. At n = 5 (p = 0.25), the difference from the shipped weight may be noise.
- **The bounded region is not exact at large hubs, for either weight.** Exactness holds on the accuracy scenarios (hubs ≤ 50), where the region often covers most of the graph.
- **N-A must change.** A pair-level certificate cannot give ring-level exactness, because the extraction step (components, then greedy peel) is not stable under restriction. The certificate must also cover extraction. For example, the region must contain each touched user's whole thresholded component (here that is about the whole graph, so no saving), or extraction must change to one that is restriction-stable. I'll take this up in N-A.

## 1. Diagnosis

The paper's "better than" claims fall into three groups:
1. **Supported wins whose attribution is wrong or missing.** The headline 0.73 vs 0.21, and recall 0.81 vs 0.53.
2. **Unscored comparisons.** The baseline lacks the scorer the proposed method has: Figs. 6–8, the large-hub rows, and per-evasion recall.
3. **Equivalence claims argued from non-significance.** The weight shape, incremental vs full, and the 250 limit.

No comparison is made against an existing published method (FRAUDAR, Spade/Spade+, incremental Leiden, a GNN). Yet the introduction and related work position the method against "rule-based systems" in general. **None of the wins is attributable to C1's discount**: the weight-shape and frontier results say so directly.

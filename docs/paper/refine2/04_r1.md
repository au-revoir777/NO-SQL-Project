## 1. Diagnosis

Section V-A credits "the candidate generator" with the 0.56 → 0.73 gain, and C1 credits "keeping hub links". Neither had a control. N1 showed that the rule baseline's recall does not depend on its cap, so the rules could not tell us whether keeping hub links matters. R1 runs that control inside the full pipeline.

## 2. Experiment (as preregistered)

- Full pipeline (pairwise detector, temporal cycle, smurfing, ensemble, tracker, shipped scorer, θ_a 0.8) in four variants: h(d) ∈ {1/ln(1+d), constant} × cap ∈ {none, 10}. Cap 10 means an attribute shared by more than 10 users contributes nothing, for every attribute kind.
- θ_e tuned per arm on seeds 301–303 (grid 0.1–1.5, ten points).
- Tested on **fresh seeds 2001–2010** × 5 scenarios = 50 paired runs.
- Reference arm: the rules with the shipped scorer.

Selected θ_e: 1/ln 0.3; 1/ln + cap 0.1 (an edge selection, but 0.1–0.3 tie on validation); constant 1.0 (interior: 1.2 scores lower, which settles R6 for the constant); constant + cap 1.0.

What R1 establishes:

1. **Keeping hub links matters, within the same pipeline.** Removing them with a cap of 10 costs 0.08 F1 overall (CI 0.05–0.12; H1 passes even under Holm with five family members: 0.002 × 5 = 0.01). It costs 0.31–0.34 F1 on hub_heavy, and hub-hiding recall falls from 29/52 to 9/52 (1/ln) and from 31/52 to 7/52 (constant). This is the isolation control C1 lacked, and it supports the "keep hub links" half of C1 on fresh seeds.
2. **The discount is not what matters.** The constant weight is numerically better than 1/ln(1+d) (+0.03, CI −0.01 to 0.07), and clearly better on drift (+0.18). Not equivalent at δ 0.05 (90% CI −0.067 to 0.010). C1's degree discount cannot be claimed as the source of any gain.
3. **Why keeping links works without a discount.** With a constant weight and θ_e = 1.0, one shared attribute gives 1.0 × decay < 1.0, so a pair is linked only when a second piece of evidence joins it: another shared attribute, or transfers. A busy hub then counts as one piece of evidence and never suffices alone. That is the property the 1/ln weight with θ_e 0.3 also has: a 50-user hub gives 0.25 and needs transfers or a second attribute. This explanation is derived from the code and the formula, not separately tested. An "evidence-count" ablation (θ_e between 1 and 2 with a constant weight, with and without transfers) would test it, and fits N2 as a candidate.
4. **Where the remaining gain over the rules comes from.** With the same cap, the pairwise pipeline and the scored rules tie overall (+0.005), but trade sharply by scenario: pairwise + cap wins on high_noise (+0.28) and loses on hub_heavy (−0.30, where the rules' cycle rule still fires). Adding "keep links" on top (H1) recovers hub_heavy. So the 0.56 → 0.73 bundle splits into:
   - the pairwise multi-detector pipeline: wins under noise, loses under hub hiding when capped;
   - keeping links: wins under hub hiding.
5. **The fresh-seed headline gap is smaller.** Proposed vs rules + scorer is 0.70 vs 0.61 (+0.09) on seeds 2001–2010, against +0.17 on 1001–1010. The final comparison (W3, seeds 2101–2120, per-method scorers) will give the number to report.

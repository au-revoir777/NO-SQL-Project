\begin{abstract}
Fraud rings share devices, cards and network addresses and move money among
themselves. Rule-based graph detectors cap the degree of shared attributes to
suppress office gateways and carrier NAT, which hides any ring behind such a
hub, and re-running whole-graph queries gets costly as the graph grows. We keep
hub links with a degree-discounted weight, detect incrementally on the region
each micro-batch touches, and score candidate rings with a calibrated,
explained model. On a deterministic simulator with ring-level ground truth and
five evasion strategies (\EScenarios{} scenarios, \ESeeds{} paired test seeds),
the pipeline reaches ring F1 \EPropF{} against \ECapTenF{} for degree-capped
Cypher rules; given the same scorer, the capped rules reach \ECapTenSF{}, so the
scorer accounts for most of the gap. Keeping hub links raises the reachable
recall on shared-attribute rings from \FrCapSMaxR{} to \FrHubSMaxR{}, while the
exact discount matters little. Incremental detection buys nothing over a 1\,s
timer-driven full pass on small graphs, but stays affordable as the graph
grows, where a 5\,s timer cannot keep up. Slow and identity-rotating rings evade every method we
tested, and all positive results are synthetic.
\end{abstract}
